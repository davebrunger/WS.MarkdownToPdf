# Three Dice Roleplaying — Quick Rules Primer
David Brunger

---
A fast cinematic roleplaying system using only three six-sided dice. This game is built around one idea:

> Every roll answers two questions:
> Did you succeed? and 
> What else happened?

You never just "fail." Something always changes.

<!-- columns: 2 -->

## What You Need
- Three six-sided dice (3d6)
- Two of one colour (Outcome Dice)
- One of a different colour (Consequence Die)

## The Core Roll
Whenever a character attempts something risky:
> Roll 2d6 (Outcome Dice) + 1d6 (Consequence Die)

### Outcome (Did it work?)
Add the two Outcome Dice together and compare to a target number.

|Outcome|Result|
|-|-|
|2 - 3|Strong Failure|
|4 - 5|Failure|
|6 - 7|Mixed Success|
|8 - 9|Success|
|10 - 12|Strong Success|

Interpretation:
- **Strong Failure**: you don't achieve your goal at a cost.
- **Failure**: you don't achieve your goal.
- **Mixed Success**: you achieve your goal imperfectly or at a cost.
- **Success**: you achieve your goal.
- **Strong Success**: you achieve your goal cleanly or gain extra benefit.

### Consequence (What else happened?)
Look only at the third die.
|Die|Effect|
|-|-|
|1|Major Threat|
|2|Minor Threat|
|3 - 4|Neutral|
|5|Minor Advantage|
|6|Major Advantage|

Interpretation:
- **Threat**: The GM introduces a complication or obstacle.
- **Advantage**: The player gains a benefit or opportunity.

<!-- columns: 2 -->

#### Example Advantages
- gain cover
- reposition
- potentially something useful
- assist an ally (+1 band to next roll)
- create an opportunity
- reduce time

#### Example Threats
- make noise
- drop equipment
- lose time
- attract attention
- enemy reposition
- equipment trouble

<!-- /columns -->

### Doubles Rule (Cinematic Moments)
If the two outcome dice match:

|Dice|Result|
|-|-|
|Double 1|Catastrophe — things go very wrong|
|Double 6|Triumph — a dramatic positive event|

## Who Rolls
Players roll for their everything. Instead of "the enemy attacks you," the question becomes: "Do you avoid being hit?"

## Characteristics
Characteristics describe what kind of person you are. They
do not add numbers. They change how difficult actions are
for you.

Each characteristic has 5 levels:

|Level|Description|
|-|-|
|1|Frail — often outmatched|
|2|Ordinary — typical adult|
|3|Capable — fit or naturally talented|
|4|Exceptional — noticeably impressive|
|5|Remarkable — people comment on it|

Before rolling, the GM decides how demanding the task is:

|Level|Description|
|-|-|
|Basic|most people can do this|
|Challenging|requires training|
|Hard|Capable — requires expertise|
|Extreme|rare ability needed|

After the roll, compare your characteristic to the demand.

|Comparison|Effect|
|-|-|
|Far Above|+2 outcome bands|
|Above|+1 outcome band|
|Equal|no change|
|Below|-1 band|
|Far Below|-2 outcome bands|

## Skills
Skills represent training and experience. Skills do not add numbers.
They improve reliability and control.

Each skill has ranks:

|Level|Description|Effect|
|-|-|-|
|0|Untrained|roll normally|
|1|Trained|reroll 1 outcome die|
|2|Professional|reroll both outcome dice|
|3|Expert|shift consequence ±1|
|4|Veteran|reroll consequence die|
|5|Master|choose consequence result|

## Difficulty
When facing an NPC, their skill modifies the result instead of rolling dice.

|NPC Skill|Effect|
|-|-|
|Weak|shift result +1 band|
|Normal|no change|
|Hard|shift result -1 band|
|Extreme|shift result -1 band and add a Threat|

## The Resolution Procedure
Every roll follows this order:

- Player describes action
- Roll 2d6 + 1d6
- Apply skill effects (rerolls/control)
- Determine outcome band
- Apply characteristic shift
- Apply NPC difficulty (if any)
- Apply consequence die
- Narrate result
